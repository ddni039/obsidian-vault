# Josef Müller-Brockmann — Wikipedia Summary

## Overview

Swiss graphic designer (1914–1996), pioneer of the International Typographic Style (Swiss Style). Author of *Grid Systems in Graphic Design* (1968/1981).

## Biography

- **Born**: May 9, 1914, Rapperswil, Switzerland
- **Died**: August 30, 1996, Unterengstringen, Switzerland
- **Education**: University of Zurich; Zurich University of the Arts (studied with Ernst Keller and Alfred Willimann)
- **Style**: International Typographic Style (Swiss Style)

## Design Philosophy

> "The grid system is an aid, not a guarantee. It permits a number of possible uses and each designer can look for a solution appropriate to his personal style. But one must learn how to use the grid; it is an art that requires practice."

Core principles:
- **Nonrepresentational abstraction**: Using geometric shapes instead of illustration
- **Visual metaphor**: Translating abstract concepts (like music) into visual form
- **Objective design**: Focus on structure over decoration
- **Grid-based systems**: Systematic approach to layout
- **Preferred typeface**: Akzidenz-Grotesk

## Tonhalle Concert Poster Series (1950–)

Most iconic work — translating musical compositions into visual form using geometric elements without illustration or embellishment. Played with visual scale, rhythm, and repetition.

## Legacy

One of the main masters of Swiss design. Systematic grid-based approach to design remains foundational in contemporary graphic design practice. His work is held in MoMA, Cooper Hewitt, and Museum of Design Zürich.

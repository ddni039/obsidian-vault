# The Elements of Typographic Style

**By Robert Bringhurst** | Second Edition (1996) | Hartley & Marks Publishers

## Core Principles

Typography exists to honor content. Letters have life and dignity of their own—"well-chosen words deserve well-chosen letters." The typographer's task is to add "a somewhat unnatural edge" to writing while ultimately disappearing.

**The typographer's responsibilities:**
1. Invite the reader into the text
2. Reveal the tenor and meaning of the text
3. Clarify structure and order
4. Link text with existing elements
5. Induce "a state of energetic repose"
6. Honor the text—and typography's own tradition

**First tactics:** Read the text before designing it. Discover the outer logic within the text's inner logic. Match visual relationships to real relationships. Choose typefaces that honor the text's character.

## Rhythm & Proportion

### Horizontal Motion

| Element | Standard |
|---------|----------|
| **Word space (justified)** | M/4 average; M/5 minimum; M/2 maximum |
| **Ideal line length** | 66 characters |
| **Satisfactory range** | 45-75 characters |
| **Letterspacing caps** | 5-10% normal; 20-25% titles |

- Use **single space** after periods (no double spaces)
- Letterspace capital strings and digits; never lowercase without reason
- Kern consistently: Av, AW, AY, 'A need reduction; f', f), f? need addition
- Use **hard rag** for ragged setting (fixed word space, minimal hyphenation)

### Vertical Motion

- Standard leading: 10/12 for body text
- Page typically holds **30-45 lines**
- Negative leading only for titles if ascenders/descenders don't collide
- Longer measures need more leading; dark faces need more than light ones

### Paragraphs & Spacing

- Opening paragraphs: flush left (no indent)
- Paragraph indents: one em standard; one en minimum
- Block quotations: add white space before and after; use one method (face, size, or indent) to distinguish
- Verse: indent or center on longest line

### Hyphenation Rules

- Avoid orphan hyphenated words at line ends
- Avoid >3 consecutive hyphenated lines
- Hyphenate proper names only as last resort
- Use hard spaces in numerical expressions (6.2 mm, page 3)

### Figures & Caps

- **Text figures** (old-style): use with lowercase or small caps
- **Titling figures** (lining): use with full caps
- Use **spaced small caps** for abbreviations (except two-letter geographical acronyms)

### Ligatures

Five basic Latin ligatures: **ff, fi, fl, ffi, ffl** — fi and fl most crucial. True italic has cursive structure with transitive serifs. Sloped romans are "calligraphic stunts."

### Type Family Hierarchy

Primary (roman lowercase) → Secondary (caps, small caps, italic) → Tertiary (bold, sloped small caps) → Quaternary through Sextary (additional weights and forms)

### Contrast Rules

Change **one parameter at a time**: size, weight, slope, or form. Don't clutter the foreground with punctuation in emphasis fonts.

## Historical Type Periods

| Period | Key Characteristics |
|--------|---------------------|
| **Renaissance (15th-16th)** | Modulated stroke; humanist axis; pen-formed terminals; large aperture; italic independent |
| **Baroque (17th)** | Variable axis; modeled serifs; moderate aperture; italic subsidiary |
| **Romantic (18th-19th)** | High contrast; vertical axis; abrupt thin serifs; small aperture |
| **Geometric Modernist (20th)** | Unmodulated stroke; serifs absent or equal weight; no italic |
| **Lyrical Modernist (20th)** | Rediscovered Renaissance form; humanist axis; italic partially liberated |

Notable figures: Nicolas Jenson (1469), Claude Garamond (1530s), Giambattista Bodoni (1803), Hermann Zapf's Palatino (1948).

## Page Design

### Proportions

Musical intervals translate to page proportions. Medieval favorites: 2:3 and 3:4. Renaissance: narrower (major/minor sixth). Golden section (φ = 1.618) appears in nature and classical architecture.

**Standard proportions:**
- ISO paper: 1:√2 (1:1.414)
- Common book: 1:1.5 to 1:1.8

### Margins

Margins must lock the textblock to the page, frame it appropriately, and leave room for thumbs. Folios work best at upper/lower outside corners.

### Textblock

For continuous reading, set in columns taller than wide. Medieval books used same proportions for page and textblock; Renaissance used polyphonic page (different proportions).

## Structural Elements

### Headings

Use as many levels as needed (A-heads through F-heads). Avoid mixing symmetrical (crossheads) and asymmetrical (sideheads) haphazardly.

### Notes

Sidenotes > footnotes for page vitality. Superscript order: * * * * * (beyond this, switch to symbolic).

# Josef Albers — Wikipedia Summary

## Overview

German-born American artist and educator (1888–1976), considered one of the most influential 20th-century art teachers in the United States. Best known for pioneering work in color theory and his seminal series *Homage to the Square*.

## Biography

- **Born**: March 19, 1888, Bottrop, Westphalia, Prussia
- **Died**: March 25, 1976, New Haven, Connecticut
- **Movement**: Geometric abstraction
- **Known for**: Abstract painting, study of color
- **Spouse**: Anni Albers (m. 1925)

## Key Career

- **1908–1913**: Worked as schoolteacher
- **1913–1915**: Trained at Königliche Kunstschule, Berlin
- **1920**: Enrolled at Weimar Bauhaus (preliminary course under Johannes Itten)
- **1922**: Joined Bauhaus faculty as stained-glass instructor
- **1925**: Married Anni Fleischmann; promoted to professor when Bauhaus moved to Dessau
- **1933**: Emigrated to US after Bauhaus closed under Nazi pressure; joined Black Mountain College
- **1950–1958**: Joined Yale University, headed design department
- **1971**: Founded the **Josef and Anni Albers Foundation**
- **1971**: First living artist to receive solo show at the Metropolitan Museum of Art

## Artworks

### Homage to the Square (begun 1949)
- Hundreds of paintings exploring chromatic interactions with nested squares
- Usually painted on Masonite using palette knife with oil colors
- Each painting: 3–4 squares of solid color, in one of four arrangements
- Format range: 406×406mm to 1.22×1.22m

### Murals
- *Two Structural Constellations* (1959) — Corning Glass Building, Manhattan
- *Two Portals* (1961) — Time & Life Building lobby
- *Manhattan* (1960s) — Pan Am Building lobby (reinstalled at MetLife 2019)

## Color Theory: Interaction of Color (1963)

Core principle: **In visual perception, a color is almost never seen as it really is.** Color is perceived differently based on its surrounding colors — context is everything.

Key concepts:
- **Relative color perception**: A color changes depending on what surrounds it
- **Simultaneous contrast**: Adjacent colors affect how each appears
- **Color exercises**: Learning through doing, not theory alone
- **1+1=3**: Color combinations produce emergent meaning beyond the sum of parts
- **Negative space as active element**: What surrounds a color is as important as the color itself

## Famous Quotes

> "In visual perception a color is almost never seen as it really is — as it physically is. This fact makes color the most relative medium in art."
> — Josef Albers, Interaction of Color

> "The purpose of all art is to accelerate the learning process."
> — Josef Albers

## Legacy

Albers developed an experiential approach to color education — students learn by doing, not by memorizing rules. His *Homage to the Square* series systematically documents how colors interact under controlled conditions. His influence extends to graphic design, interior design, and digital color systems.
